Foreword
More than ever before, in the history of the District,
the people of Kalomo are experiencing extremely
harsh climatic and whether conditions that defy
individual efforts to overcome. In the recent past,
annual rainfall patterns have been changing,
characterized by prolonged droughts , extreme
heat, and flush floods, often leaving devastating
effects on the livelihoods of our people. A majority
of our small-scale farmers directly depend on rain-
fed agriculture for survival. Livestock and crop
yields have been negatively impacted by the
adverse weather patterns. Water scarcity, livestock
diseases, shortage of manpower (extension workers), deplorable road
infrastructure, all amplify the magnitude of the problems we face in Kalomo.
However, such challenges-though enormous-will be overcome when we
unite and work together.
Collectively, we can adapt and courageously embark on sustainable
farming practices, water conservation, reforestation, wildlife conservation,
and several other interventions that have the potential to re-position Kalomo
district towards a “Climate Resilient District that is economically prosperous,
and anchored on sustainable use of local resources.”
Therefore, the Kalomo District Integrated Development Plan (2021-2030) is a
vital step towards a collective effort in addressing many local challenges
that hinder local economic transformation (and job creation), human and
Social development, environmental sustainabilty, and good governance.
True Integration implies collective responsibility. Together we overcome
problems we face. Therefore, I want to commend all the participants of the
IDP preparation process in ensuring a consultative approach was adopted.
I would like to thank all Cooperating Partners and the local private sector (all
local business houses), NGOs, and churches, and other stakeholders for
supporting this Integrated Development Plan. Let us all support its successful
implementation.
COY MAKAYA
COUNCIL CHAIRPERSON

1.0 PART ONE
1.1 Introduction
The integrated development planning process is regulated by the Urban
and Regional Planning Act No. 3 of 2015. It provides that each Local
authority adopts a single, inclusive and integrated plan for the development
of each individual district. This document will be an overall framework for the
development of the district that will guide and inform decision making by all
levels of government and all stakeholders. Kalomo District has followed
through the guidelines for the development of a district integrated
development plan with a view of integrating the social, economic,
environmental, spatial, infrastructural, institutional and organizational
development, and the provision of social services aimed at alleviating
poverty and improving the quality of life for Kalomo people.
The methodology of preparing this plan for Kalomo District was based on

1.3 Spatial location of the district
Kalomo District is located in Southern Province and lies within Latitude 160
and 170 south and Longitude 260 and 270 North and along the Lusaka –
Livingstone Road (T1) and is approximately 120 km north of Livingstone town
and approximately 400 km south of Lusaka.
The district lies at 1300 m above sea level and has a total surface area of
8,374 km² and shares borders with five (5) districts, namely; Itezhi-Tezhi in
Central Province, and in Southern Province, Namwala in the North, Choma
in the northeast, Kazungula and Zimba in the South. The district has a total of
20 wards as shown in figure 2.

1.4 Demographics and population growth analysis
This section analyzes the district population in terms of size and future
projections at 10-year intervals. Kalomo District boundary and the
population was reduced following the subdivision of the district to create
Zimba District in 2013, where Mapatizya Constituency became Zimba
District.
Kalomo District had a total population of 258,570 in 2010 as compared to
169,503 in 2000 indicating a 65% increase. In 2010, the population of males
was 125,767 and 132,803 females representing 48.6% and 51.4% respectively.
However, after the subdivision of the district, the population was reduced to
188,693 from 258,570 segregated as 91,929 males representing 48.7 % and
96,764 females representing 51.3 %.
Even with the subdivision of Kalomo District boundary, the population
continues to be the third-highest in the province comparing with the
population statistics of other districts. Choma has the highest population
which stands at 247,860 followed by Mazabuka at 230,972 then Kalomo at
188,693 (CSO, 2010).

1.4.3 Population density
Figure 8 shows population density map for Kalomo district. Population
density is a very useful indicator of human settlements per square kilometre
and the extent to which local authorities and cooperating partners need to
respond to challenges that come with sparsely or densely populated areas.
The population density for Kalomo district is 33.1 people per square kilometre
(CSO, 2010) as compared to other districts like Choma whose population
density stands at 34 people per square kilometre (CSO,2010).
An anlysis at ward level shows that Choonga ward has the highest
population density at 48.5. This can be attributed to the ward being along
the line of the rail where most of the economic activities and services are.
Mayoba ward on the hand has the lowest population density at 7 due to it
being predominatly private farms owned by 5 commercial farmers.
Siachitema has the second-highest population density. This is attributed to
the fact that Siachitema was the subdistrict centre and the mission
headquarters for Brethren in Christ Church with a number of social amenities
were provided by the government.

1.5 Population trends on land use and Spatial Development Patterns
This section highlights the land use and spatial development patterns of the
district. Kalomo district is predominantly a rural set-up with only 8% of the
population residing in the Urban area of Mwaata ward.
1.5.1 Settlements and Land Tenure
Kalomo district is one of the oldest towns in Zambia where white settlers
settled. The district was the first administrative capital of the British South
African Company’s in North-Western Rhodesia from 1902 to 1907. The
coming of the white settlers influenced the settlement patterns of the district.
Most of the development was located on the plateau passing through
Kalomo Central Constituency. Most of the development which included
infrastructure and farm settlement was along the line of rail. This is evidenced
by the cadastral maps of the district where most farms are located. The
plateau provides good soils for farming and these farms are in a radius of 11
kilometres on each side of the rail line.
Most of the people found in the district are arable farmers and also
pastoralist. This has a bearing on the settlement pattern in the district. In the
recent past, there has been pressure on the land and encroachments on
protected forests and game management areas. Most of these protected
forest areas and game management areas are in the northern part of the
district in chief Chikanta chiefdom. Figure 9 shows the level of
encroachment in the protected areas. Chikanta small settlements are on
